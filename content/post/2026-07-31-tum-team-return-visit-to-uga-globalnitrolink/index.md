From **24–31 July 2026**, a team from the **TUM School of Life Sciences**, travelled to the **University of Georgia (UGA)** in Athens, GA, for the reciprocal leg of the **[GlobalNitroLink](/project/globalnitrolink/)** exchange. [**Prof. Kang Yu**](/author/prof.-dr.-kang-yu/) from the TUM Precision Agriculture Lab, **Dr. Martin Mittermayer** and PhD student **Ludwig Hagn** from the TUM Chair of Organic Agriculture and Agronomy, made the journey from Munich via Atlanta to Athens for a week of joint seminars, field visits, lab tours, and planning meetings with our UGA partners.

The visit was carried out under the framework of **GlobalNitroLink**, a TUM–UGA international exchange project funded through the **BayIntAn** programme of the **Bavarian Research Alliance (BayFOR)**, which supports new research partnerships between Bavarian universities and leading international institutions. It followed the [**joint UGA–TUM seminar at Weihenstephan in June 2026**](/post/2026-06-10-uga-tum-joint-precision-agriculture-seminar/), when Prof. Leonardo Bastos and Prof. Lorena Lacerda visited TUM — completing the two reciprocal research visits at the heart of the project.

## Day 1 — Campus, IIPA, and International Partnerships

The first full day (**27 July**) began with a tour of the UGA campus, Bookstore, Library, the Innovation Distric, **Miller Plant Sciences Building**, and the **Institute for Integrative Precision Agriculture (IIPA)** — UGA's new flagship initiative bringing together engineering, data science, and the agricultural and environmental sciences.

![The TUM GlobalNitroLink team at the Miller Plant Sciences Building on the UGA campus.](team_miller_plant_sciences.jpeg)

![The IIPA lecture room, where much of the week's exchange took place.](iipa_lecture_room.jpeg)

A central working meeting brought together the TUM team with the **UGA Office of International Programs** (Victoria McMaken and Todd Applegate), the **Director of the IIPA, Prof. George Vellidis**, and the **Office of Global Engagement** (Prof. Martin Kagel, Dr. Leah Peck, and Dr. Yana Cornish) to explore opportunities for research and educational collaboration — from joint projects to student exchange.

![Meeting with IIPA Director Prof. George Vellidis and the UGA Office of International Programs at the UGA Conference Center — the venue for ICPA 2028.](uga_office_intl_programs.jpeg)

![Athens is home to UGA's iconic Sanford Stadium — a fitting backdrop to the campus tour.](sanford_stadium.jpeg)

## Day 2 — Iron Horse Farm Research Station

**28 July**, the team visited UGA's **Iron Horse Farm** research station. The team toured the station's facilities, machinery, and drone and sensor technology, and walked the **on-farm nitrogen and irrigation trials** in soybean, maize, and cotton.

![Touring the Iron Horse Farm research station with our UGA hosts.](iron_horse_farm.jpeg)

![On-farm nitrogen and nitrous-oxide (N₂O) trials in soybean and maize.](n2o_soybean_maize_trial.jpeg)

![Walking the cotton field trials at Iron Horse Farm.](cotton_field_trial.jpeg)

UGA PhD students presented their work in an afternoon seminar, sparking discussion on latest results from field trials and novel modeling work, **linking satellite-derived crop indicators with nitrogen and irrigation decision-making and yield prediction** — the core scientific theme of GlobalNitroLink.

![Closing group photo after the afternoon student seminar.](iron_horse_group_photo.jpeg)

## Day 3 — Joint TUM–UGA Seminar and Lab Tours

The morning of **29 July** was a joint seminar day. The TUM team gave three talks — by **Prof. Kang Yu**, **Dr. Martin Mittermayer**, and **Ludwig Hagn** — on satellite- and sensor-based crop monitoring, digital yield mapping and nutrient management, and UAV crop monitoring at TUM.

![Prof. Kang Yu presenting the TUM Precision Agriculture Lab's work on satellite-based crop nitrogen indicators.](kang_yu_presentation.jpeg)

Four UGA faculty members — **Profs. Liang Dong**, **Xin Zhang**, **Zhihang Song**, and **Tao Liu** — presented their research spanning wearable plant sensors, agricultural robotics, controlled-environment phenotyping, and crop sensing.

## Connections and Next Steps

The visit closed the loop on the two reciprocal exchanges of GlobalNitroLink and turned the conversations started in Weihenstephan into concrete plans on the ground in Athens. The shared focus — integrating **multi-temporal satellite crop indicators** with **yield monitoring** for more robust and transferable **nitrogen management** — gives TUM and UGA a natural foundation for continued joint research, comparison of precision N-management approaches across contrasting agro-climatic regions, and future student exchange.
