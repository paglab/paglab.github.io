abstract: Swath-based remote sensing data often exhibit spatial discontinuities after
  mapping to latitude-longitude grids at a regular spacing due to uneven sampling
  caused by varying viewing angles and limitations of the conventional center point-based
  gridding method (CPGrid). A more complex area-weighted gridding method can enhance
  spatial continuity, but it requires geometric calculations for each grid and is
  computationally intensive, especially for large-scale satellite imagery. To balance
  accuracy and efficiency, we proposed a probabilistic area-weighted gridding method
  (PAGrid), which approximates area-weighting by aggregating results from multiple
  randomized spatial perturbations. The performance of PAGrid was evaluated using
  all available Sentinel-3A and 3B observations in 2022 over Germany. Using the canopy
  absorption coefficient by chlorophyll in the red-edge band ($\alpha$RE) as a test
  variable, we generated 8-day composites and compared results from CPGrid and PAGrid
  methods. PAGrid increased the median percentage of valid grid cells from 85\% to
  93\% and reduced temporal fluctuations by 21\% compared to CPGrid. Additionally,
  PAGrid improved the correlation (R2) between Sentinel-3A and 3B $\alpha$RE from
  0.73 to 0.84, indicating enhanced data consistency. These improvements indicate
  that PAGrid is a practical and efficient approach for generating consistent and
  continuous gridded time series from swath-based satellite observations.
